\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Autonomous pinpoint landing on celestial bodies represents one of the most critical and challenging disciplines within aerospace guidance, navigation, and control (GNC) \cite{blackmore2016autonomous}. The terminal powered descent phase demands real-time execution of highly constrained, non-linear control maneuvers where thruster modulation must simultaneously eliminate residual translational kinetic energy, regulate attitude orientation, counteract gravitational acceleration, and nullify impact velocities within stringent dispersion ellipses \cite{acikmese2013flight}. Compounding this dynamical complexity is the substantial variation in vehicle inertial properties: as propellant is consumed at high mass flow rates, the total vehicle mass and moment of inertia continuously decrease, altering the control authority and open-loop poles of the system in real time.

Classical aerospace landing architectures typically rely on deterministic trajectory optimization routines---such as the Apollo quadratic guidance polynomial or convexified guidance solvers---coupled with gain-scheduled linear controllers \cite{acikmese2013flight}. While mathematically rigorous, such paradigms necessitate high-fidelity dynamical models, computationally demanding onboard numerical solvers, and extensive manual tuning of stability margins to accommodate plant parameter variations. In scenarios marked by unmodeled atmospheric turbulence, ground-effect disturbances, or abrupt parametric shifts in center-of-mass and inertia, classical fixed-gain architectures exhibit degraded robustness and elevated fuel expenditure \cite{vankampen2025lecture}.

To transcend these limitations, bio-inspired intelligence and machine learning paradigms have garnered significant interest in flight control \cite{vankampen2025lecture}. Biological organisms achieve versatile motor control and dynamic equilibrium through continuous sensorimotor adaptation, processing sensory state feedback to emit coordinated muscular actions via trial-and-error reinforcement \cite{sutton2018reinforcement}. Reinforcement Learning (RL) formalizes this biological paradigm as a mathematical optimization framework, enabling an artificial agent to synthesize closed-loop control policies directly through interaction with high-fidelity physical environments \cite{sutton2018reinforcement, lillicrap2015continuous}.

Within the scope of the AE4350 curriculum, this technical investigation evaluates the design, training, and empirical performance of a deep continuous reinforcement learning controller based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) \cite{schulman2017proximal}. The controller is tasked with executing soft pinpoint landings for an underactuated, mass-varying lunar lander implemented in an augmented Box2D multi-body physics environment \cite{towers2023gymnasium}. The report is structured as follows: \Cref{sec:system_description} defines the coordinate frames, non-linear time-varying flight dynamics, actuator models, and operational constraints. \Cref{sec:rl_controller_design} formulates the continuous Markov Decision Process, Actor-Critic neural network architecture, surrogate loss mechanics, and continuous exploration strategy. \Cref{sec:learning_effect} validates the convergence behavior and learning progression across training epochs. \Cref{sec:sensitivity_analysis} presents a parametric sensitivity study exploring learning rates, planning horizons, and entropy regularization. \Cref{sec:results} investigates the synthesized flight trajectories, control strategies, and touchdown statistics, followed by concluding remarks and future outlook in \Cref{sec:conclusion}.
