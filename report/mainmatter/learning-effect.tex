\section{The Learning Effect and Training Convergence}
\label{sec:learning_effect}

\subsection{Nominal Training Progress}
To evaluate the learning process, the nominal PPO agent was trained for $300{,}000$ environment timesteps across 4 parallel environments using the baseline hyperparameters ($\alpha = 3 \times 10^{-4}$, $\gamma = 0.99$, $c_{\text{ent}} = 0.01$). Every $10{,}000$ steps, the policy was evaluated over 15 test episodes without exploration noise. The resulting learning curves are presented in \Cref{fig:nominal_learning_curve}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{figures/nominal_learning_curve.pdf}
    \caption{Training progression of the nominal PPO agent over 300k timesteps: mean evaluation reward with $\pm 1\sigma$ confidence band (top), safe landing success rate (bottom-left), and mean remaining propellant at touchdown (bottom-right).}
    \label{fig:nominal_learning_curve}
\end{figure}

The training history clearly displays three distinct learning phases:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Initial Exploration and Crashes (0 to 50k steps):} At the beginning of training, the agent knows nothing about the environment and takes random actions. It struggles to control the lander's attitude, frequently flipping upside down, burning fuel erratically, or crashing into the ground at high speeds ($100\%$ crash rate). The evaluation return drops to a low of $-250.93 \pm 82.26$ at 40k steps.
    \item \textbf{Attitude Stabilization and Rapid Breakthrough (50k to 100k steps):} Between 50k and 100k steps, a dramatic improvement in performance is observed. The agent discovers that using the side thrusters keeps the vehicle upright ($\theta \approx 0$) and that firing the main engine slows down its vertical fall. As a result, the landing success rate jumps rapidly from $0\%$ to $86.7\%$, and the average reward surges from $-78.38$ up to $+194.27$.
    \item \textbf{Fine-Tuning and Propellant Optimization (100k to 300k steps):} Beyond 100k steps, the agent consistently achieves rewards above the environment solved threshold of $+200$. In this phase, the agent fine-tunes its throttle commands to touch down softly on both legs while minimizing unnecessary fuel burn. The policy reaches a peak reward of $+224.14 \pm 32.81$ with a $100\%$ landing success rate, conserving approximately $54.1\%$ of its initial fuel reserve at touchdown.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Learning Stability and Variance Reduction}
The shaded region in \Cref{fig:nominal_learning_curve} represents the $\pm 1\sigma$ standard deviation across evaluation episodes. During the breakthrough phase (at 70k steps), the variance is large ($\sigma = 161.40$) because the agent is still transitioning between failed crashes and occasional successful landings. As training progresses to 150k steps and beyond, the standard deviation shrinks down to $\sigma = 17.98$ in some points, but it still remains significantly large during most of the steps. This lack of tightening of the confidence band suggests that the PPO controller with nominal hyperparameters struggles to converge to a stable, highly consistent landing strategy. This is likely caused by the added complexity of the mass-varying system.
