\section{Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:sensitivity_analysis}

To systematically investigate algorithm sensitivity and identify the critical drivers of policy synthesis, a comprehensive parametric study was conducted across three foundational hyperparameters: learning rate $\alpha$, discount factor $\gamma$, and entropy coefficient $c_{\text{ent}}$. Each configuration was evaluated using automated convergence criteria, where convergence is defined as achieving a mean evaluation return $\bar{R} \ge 190.0$ with a landing success rate $\ge 90\%$. The empirical convergence statistics are compiled in \Cref{tab:sensitivity_summary}, and the comparative convergence speeds are depicted in \Cref{fig:convergence_barplot}.

\subsection{Influence of Optimization Learning Rate \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}}
The optimization learning rate dictates the gradient step magnitude during Adam network updates. As shown in \Cref{tab:sensitivity_summary}, increasing $\alpha$ from $1.0 \times 10^{-4}$ to $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ monotonically accelerates convergence, reducing required environment interactions from $190{,}000$ steps ($224.9\,\si{s}$) to $70{,}000$ steps ($81.2\,\si{s}$). 

However, an aggressive rate ($\alpha = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}$) induces minor policy jitter in steady-state hover, resulting in slightly lower asymptotic fuel conservation ($40.8\%$ vs. $49.1\%$). The intermediate rate $\alpha = 3.0 \times 10^{-4}$ delivers optimal stability and rapid convergence ($90{,}000$ steps) without incurring gradient pathology.

\subsection{Influence of Planning Horizon and Discount Factor \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}}
The discount factor $\gamma$ defines the geometric decay of future reward signals. A dramatic performance divergence is observed across values:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item \textbf{Myopic Failure ($\gamma = 0.95$):} The agent completely fails to synthesize a landing policy, exhibiting zero successful touchdowns ($0.0\%$) and an evaluation return of $-115.76$. Over a typical $300$-step descent trajectory, the terminal $+100.0$ touchdown reward at step $t=200$ is discounted by $\gamma^{200} = 0.95^{200} \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-5}$. Consequently, the distant terminal objective provides negligible gradient signal; the agent greedily exploits local potential gains by burning maximum thrust immediately, causing premature propellant exhaustion.
    \item \textbf{Far-Sighted Fuel Economy ($\gamma = 0.999$):} Expanding the planning horizon to $\gamma = 0.999$ increases asymptotic propellant conservation to a remarkable $64.87\%$. Because long-term cumulative fuel penalties are accurately integrated across the entire trajectory, the agent learns an optimal ballistic coast phase followed by a high-efficiency gravity-turn terminal deceleration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Influence of Policy Entropy Regularization \texorpdfstring{$c_{\text{ent}}$}{c_ent}}
As established in \Cref{sec:rl_controller_design}, continuous action exploration requires sustaining policy distribution entropy. The empirical results substantiate this theoretical requirement:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item At $c_{\text{ent}} = 0.0$, the absence of entropy regularization allows the Gaussian covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\theta$ to prematurely shrink, locking the policy into a conservative, high-thrust strategy that converges slowly ($120{,}000$ steps) and burns excessive fuel ($31.3\%$ remaining).
    \item Introducing $c_{\text{ent}} = 0.05$ actively incentivizes continuous action exploration, accelerating convergence to $90{,}000$ steps ($482$ episodes) and achieving superior terminal returns ($196.65$) and higher propellant conservation ($42.8\%$).
\end{itemize}
