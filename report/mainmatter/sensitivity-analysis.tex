
\section{Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:sensitivity_analysis}

Hyperparameter selection strongly influences the learning speed, stability, and final performance of reinforcement learning controllers \cite{vankampen2025lecture}. To evaluate the sensitivity of the PPO agent, three critical parameters were systematically analyzed: the learning rate $\alpha$, the discount factor $\gamma$, and the entropy coefficient $c_{\text{ent}}$.

A certified convergence criterion was defined to evaluate training: achieving a mean evaluation reward $\bar{R} \ge 190.0$ with a landing success rate $\ge 90\%$. The comparative results are summarized in \Cref{tab:sensitivity_summary}, with the individual learning trajectories illustrated in \Cref{fig:sensitivity_lr,fig:sensitivity_gamma,fig:sensitivity_entropy}, and the overall convergence comparison presented in \Cref{fig:convergence_barplot}.

\subsection{Influence of the Learning Rate \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}}
The learning rate $\alpha$ controls the step size taken by the optimizer when updating the weights of the Actor and Critic neural networks. Three values were tested: $1.0 \times 10^{-4}$, $3.0 \times 10^{-4}$, and $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$.

\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{figures/sensitivity_learning_rate.pdf}
    \caption{Evaluation reward progression for different learning rates $\alpha$, with their respective $\pm 1\sigma$ confidence bands.}
    \label{fig:sensitivity_lr}
\end{figure}

As shown in \Cref{fig:sensitivity_lr}, increasing the learning rate directly speeds up initial policy acquisition:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item With $\alpha = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}$, the agent reaches convergence the fastest, requiring only $70{,}000$ steps ($383$ episodes). However, large weight updates introduce slight jitter during fine hover control, resulting in slightly lower fuel remaining ($40.8\%$).
    \item With $\alpha = 1.0 \times 10^{-4}$, training is very stable and achieves a high final reward ($210.61$), but requires $190{,}000$ steps to converge.
    \item The nominal value $\alpha = 3.0 \times 10^{-4}$ provides a balanced compromise between fast learning ($90{,}000$ steps) and smooth control.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Influence of the Discount Factor \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}}
The discount factor $\gamma$ defines the agent's effective planning horizon, determining how heavily future rewards are weighted against immediate rewards \cite{sutton2018reinforcement}. Three values were compared: $0.95$, $0.99$, and $0.999$.

\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{figures/sensitivity_gamma.pdf}
    \caption{Evaluation reward progression for different discount factors $\gamma$.}
    \label{fig:sensitivity_gamma}
\end{figure}

As illustrated in \Cref{fig:sensitivity_gamma}, the discount factor proved to be the most influential hyperparameter in this study:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item \textbf{Complete Failure with $\gamma = 0.95$:} The agent completely failed to learn a successful landing policy ($0\%$ success rate, $-115.76$ average reward). In a typical descent lasting $200$ to $300$ steps, discounting by $\gamma = 0.95$ reduces the value of the $+100$ touchdown reward at step $200$ to $0.95^{200} \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-5}$, making the final landing goal virtually invisible to the agent during flight. As a result, the agent acts myopically, firing full thrusters early to maximize immediate position shaping and running out of fuel before touching down.
    \item \textbf{Far-Sighted Planning with $\gamma = 0.999$:} By extending the planning horizon, the agent accounts for the cumulative fuel penalty over the entire descent. This encourages the agent to discover a fuel-efficient ballistic coast followed by a timely braking burn, conserving an impressive $64.9\%$ of propellant at touchdown (\Cref{tab:sensitivity_summary}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Influence of the Entropy Coefficient \texorpdfstring{$c_{\text{ent}}$}{c_ent}}
As explained in \Cref{sec:continuous_exploration}, the entropy coefficient $c_{\text{ent}}$ governs the exploration-exploitation balance by penalizing premature collapse of the Gaussian policy variance $\sigma^2$. Three values were evaluated: $0.0$, $0.01$, and $0.05$.

\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{figures/sensitivity_ent_coef.pdf}
    \caption{Evaluation reward progression for different entropy regularization coefficients $c_{\text{ent}}$.}
    \label{fig:sensitivity_entropy}
\end{figure}

As shown in \Cref{fig:sensitivity_entropy}, the entropy regularization coefficient directly governs exploration during training:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item With $c_{\text{ent}} = 0.0$ (no entropy bonus), the action variance shrinks quickly, locking the agent into an overly conservative policy that converges more slowly ($120{,}000$ steps) and burns more propellant ($31.3\%$ remaining).
    \item With $c_{\text{ent}} = 0.05$, active exploration across continuous throttle combinations allows the agent to discover efficient landing maneuvers faster, reaching convergence in $90{,}000$ steps with $100\%$ landing success and $42.8\%$ fuel remaining.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Overall Convergence Comparison}

\begin{figure}[htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/convergence_comparison_barplot.pdf}
    \caption{Comparison of training steps and mean return to achieve certified convergence across hyperparameter configurations.}
    \label{fig:convergence_barplot}
\end{figure}
A comparison of the total environment steps required to reach certified convergence and the corresponding final mean reward is shown in \Cref{fig:convergence_barplot}. The results demonstrate that an adequate planning horizon ($\gamma \ge 0.99$) combined with active continuous exploration ($c_{\text{ent}} > 0$) is essential for reliable convergence. Lastly, a learning rate of $\alpha = 3\times 10^{-4}$ proves to be the best compromise between control performance and convergence steps.
