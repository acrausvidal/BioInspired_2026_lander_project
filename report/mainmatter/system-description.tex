\section{System Description and Physical Modeling}
\label{sec:system_description}

\subsection{Environment and Coordinate Frames}
The simulation is built on the 2D continuous Lunar Lander environment in Gymnasium \cite{towers2023gymnasium}, powered by the Box2D multi-body physics engine operating at $50\,\si{Hz}$. To describe the vehicle motion, two reference frames are defined:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item \textbf{Inertial Frame} $\mathcal{F}_I$: Fixed to the lunar surface with its origin $(x=0, y=0)$ located at the center of the landing pad. The $x$-axis points horizontally to the right, and the $y$-axis points vertically upward opposite to gravity ($g = 10.0\,\si{m/s^2}$).
    \item \textbf{Body Frame} $\mathcal{F}_B$: Fixed to the lander's center of mass, with its longitudinal axis aligned with the main engine thrust line. The attitude of the lander is defined by the pitch angle $\theta \in [-\pi, \pi]$, where $\theta = 0$ corresponds to an upright vehicle.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dynamic Mass and Propellant Depletion Model}
In standard benchmark environments, the lander's mass is kept constant throughout the episode. However, real rocket descent stages experience substantial mass loss due to propellant consumption. To incorporate this physical effect, a dynamic mass model was implemented:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item The lander starts each episode with a maximum fuel capacity of $F_0 = 100.0\,\text{units}$.
    \item Operating the main engine consumes up to $0.40\,\text{units}$ of fuel per frame, while the side thrusters consume up to $0.05\,\text{units}$ per frame.
    \item As fuel is burned, the body density $\rho(t)$ decreases linearly from its initial wet density $\rho_0 = 5.0\,\si{kg/m^2}$ (total mass $\approx 4.82\,\si{kg}$) to its dry density $\rho_{\text{dry}} = 2.5\,\si{kg/m^2}$ (dry mass $\approx 2.41\,\si{kg}$):
    \begin{equation}
        \rho(t) = \rho_{\text{dry}} + (\rho_0 - \rho_{\text{dry}}) \cdot \frac{F(t)}{F_0}.
        \label{eq:density_scaling}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
At every simulation step, the physics engine updates the vehicle mass and moment of inertia. As a consequence, the lander becomes $50\%$ lighter when empty, meaning that the same engine thrust produces twice the acceleration near the end of the descent.

\subsection{State Space, Action Space, and Reward Structure}
\textbf{State Space:} The state observation is a 9-dimensional vector containing the lander's kinematic states, landing leg ground contacts, and the remaining fuel fraction:
\begin{equation}
    s = \left[ x,\, y,\, v_x,\, v_y,\, \theta,\, \omega,\, \text{left leg contact},\, \text{right leg contact},\, \frac{F(t)}{F_0} \right].
    \label{eq:state_vector}
\end{equation}
All continuous quantities are normalized to roughly $[-1, 1]$ to facilitate neural network training.

\textbf{Action Space:} The action space is continuous and consists of two control inputs $u = [u_1, u_2] \in [-1, 1]^2$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item \textbf{Main Engine ($u_1$):} If $u_1 \le 0$, the main engine is off. If $u_1 > 0$, the engine fires with a continuous throttle between $50\%$ and $100\%$ ($u_{\text{main}} = 0.5 + 0.5 u_1$), producing up to $13.0\,\si{N}$ of upward thrust.
    \item \textbf{Side Thrusters ($u_2$):} If $|u_2| \le 0.5$, the side thrusters are off. If $|u_2| > 0.5$, the corresponding thruster fires with a directional throttle between $50\%$ and $100\%$ ($u_{\text{side}} = \text{sign}(u_2)|u_2|$), producing up to $0.6\,\si{N}$ to control attitude and horizontal translation.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reward Function:} The reward function is formulated to guide the lander smoothly to the pad:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,label=$\bullet$]
    \item \textbf{Shaping Rewards:} Positive rewards are given for moving closer to the center of the landing pad, decreasing speed, and maintaining an upright orientation ($|\theta| \approx 0$).
    \item \textbf{Ground Contact:} $+10$ points for each leg in stable contact with the surface.
    \item \textbf{Fuel and Thruster Penalties:} Small negative penalties are applied whenever engines fire ($-0.30$ for main, $-0.03$ for side) along with an additional penalty of $-0.50$ per unit of fuel consumed to incentivize fuel efficiency.
    \item \textbf{Terminal Rewards:} $+100$ points for achieving a safe soft landing on both legs, and $-100$ points for crashing into the terrain, flying out of bounds, or depleting all fuel before landing. An episode scoring $\ge 200$ is considered successfully solved.
\end{itemize}
